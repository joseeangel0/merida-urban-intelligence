# 4. Key KPIs and spatial analysis

The economic layer retained 56,667 DENUE establishments located inside Mérida's
526 urban AGEBs. Of these, 19,342 belong to retail sector 46. The spatial join
agreed with DENUE's reported AGEB for 99.81% of establishments; the small number
of disagreements is consistent with points close to geographic boundaries and
is auditable through `cvegeo_reported`.

The warehouse exposes total businesses, business density per km², businesses per
1,000 residents, retail density, service density, and the dominant economic
sector and its share. Density and per-capita measures answer different questions:
the former describes spatial concentration, whereas the latter compares economic
presence with resident population. Per-capita values in AGEBs with fewer than 100
residents require particular caution.

Spatial autocorrelation uses the 483 AGEBs in the contiguous Mérida city core.
The primary neighbourhood rule is row-standardised Queen contiguity, with its
single island connected to the nearest AGEB. Row-standardised KNN-6 provides a
sensitivity comparison. Global Moran's I and Local Moran cluster inference use
999 permutations and α = 0.05. The implemented workflow reports I, its expected
value, permutation z-score, pseudo p-value and sample size for every indicator,
then maps significant HH, LL, HL and LH clusters.

Numerical Moran and LISA results are generated by
`notebooks/32_global_moran_lisa.ipynb` after the integrated warehouse is loaded.
They are intentionally not stated here before that warehouse-dependent execution.
Any significant pattern represents spatial association under the chosen weights;
it does not demonstrate that business, population, economic participation, or
crime causes conditions in neighboring AGEBs.
